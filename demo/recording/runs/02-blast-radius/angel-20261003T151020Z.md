The changeorder subcommands are used to manage change orders.

A ChangeSet names a change within one Space and locks the Units it is open on. A ChangeOrder names
the change itself: the ChangeSets a change creates as it is promoted from Space to Space all carry
the same ChangeOrderID, which is what lets a fleet be asked where a change has landed.

A ChangeOrder resides in a Space, by convention the one holding the changes to promote, and is
bounded by a start and an end Tag created with it. It selects where it is headed with a Filter over
Spaces, and names the Link UpdateType to follow (UpgradeUnit, the clone lineage).

Creating one decides what the change is; promoting one takes it into a target:

  cub unit update --patch --space my-dev --where "Slug LIKE '%'" \

# Approval request for the approver: outage 2, shop Degraded on prod-us-1 and prod-us-2

The shop is still down on both US clusters. Three change orders wait for your approval on `chaos-management/record`; nothing is published.

## What broke
- **Where:** `web` is 0 of 2 available on prod-us-1 and prod-us-2. prod-eu and staging are Healthy, and `api` and the platform are fine everywhere.
- **Cause:** at 15:07:42Z someone applied a ClusterProfile `shop-lockdown` by hand (kubectl) on the management cluster. It is not in the record.
- **Effect:** it selects every cluster labelled `region: us` and delivers a deny-all-ingress NetworkPolicy `lockdown` into namespace `shop`, from a ConfigMap `default/shop-lockdown`. Since then web's readiness probe times out (`urlopen error timed out`).
- **Why it was possible:** the management cluster had no admission policy, so anyone with access could add a profile beside the record's.

## The changes, in the order to release them

**A. Fix — `chaos-management/outage-2-a-take-over-shop-lockdown`**

A new unit `withdraw-shop-lockdown` takes over the hand-applied objects and makes them harmless:
```diff
 kind: ClusterProfile
 metadata: {name: shop-lockdown}
 spec:
   clusterSelector:
     matchLabels:
-      region: us
+      sveltos.confighub.com/withdrawn: shop-lockdown   # no cluster has it
-  policyRefs: [{kind: ConfigMap, name: shop-lockdown, namespace: default}]
+  policyRefs: []
+  stopMatchingBehavior: WithdrawPolicies
 ---
 kind: ConfigMap   # default/shop-lockdown
-data: {policy.yaml: <NetworkPolicy lockdown, deny all ingress>}
+data: {policy.yaml: ""}
```
Sveltos then withdraws the NetworkPolicy from both clusters and web becomes ready. A also carries the policy of step B without its binding, so it enforces nothing yet.

**B. Prevention — `chaos-management/outage-2-b-profiles-only-from-record`**

The unit `guardrails` gains the binding that switches the policy on:
```diff
+kind: ValidatingAdmissionPolicyBinding
+metadata: {name: profiles-only-from-record}
+spec: {policyName: profiles-only-from-record, validationActions: ["Deny"]}
```
The policy refuses any create, update or delete of a ClusterProfile, and any create or update of a Profile, on the management cluster. Two identities are exempt, each for one job:
- `system:serviceaccount:projectsveltos:register-mgmt-cluster`, as which Sveltos applies the record's releases: any write.
- `system:serviceaccount:projectsveltos:addon-controller`, which adds and removes its finalizer and labels: only an update that leaves `spec` unchanged.

Nobody else is exempt, including cluster admins and the root profile `chaos-management`.

**C. Clean-up — `chaos-management/outage-2-c-remove-shop-lockdown`**

The unit `withdraw-shop-lockdown` is emptied, so the record deletes the taken-over ClusterProfile and ConfigMap. The empty unit can be deleted afterwards.

**Why this order:** A ends the outage and does not depend on the policy being right. B goes before C so that C, a profile deletion by the record under the policy, also shows that the policy lets the record through. Each release contains the earlier ones, so they must not be released out of order.

## What checked it
- **Cause:** on prod-eu, where the profile does not match, web pods of the same age are ready; on the US clusters readiness failed from the moment `lockdown` appeared.
- **What each change order pins:** I read the revision tags. A pins the take-over and the unbound policy, B adds the binding, C the emptied unit.
- **Policy accepted:** the sandbox API server took the policy with no type-check warnings.
- **Preview of the real policy is undecided:** all five known cases (`chaos-policies/known-cases-management`) come back `unknown`. The policy decides on the requesting user and on `oldObject`, which the preview cannot supply.
- **Preview with the two exemptions removed:** all five are denied (the outage's `shop-lockdown`, a hand copy of a record profile, forged owner marks, a Profile, the root profile changed by hand). With no policy, all five are allowed today.
- **Not tested anywhere:** that the two exempt identities are let through. I identified them from the management cluster's Sveltos registration (a renewed token, and `register-mgmt-cluster` is bound to cluster-admin) and from the field managers on the record's profiles.

## What could go wrong
- **A does not neutralise the profile.** I assume Sveltos's take-over overrides the fields kubectl set. If it does not, web stays Degraded after A; I will watch live status and say so.
- **B exempts the wrong identity.** If Sveltos writes profiles as some other account, the record can no longer change ClusterProfiles. Running profiles keep working, and the binding can still be withdrawn through the record because it is not a ClusterProfile. C is the test.
- **B blocks legitimate hand work.** Bootstrap or onboarding scripts that `kubectl apply` profiles, a change to the root profile, and EventTrigger-generated profiles (none exist today) will be refused.
- **B does not cover everything.** Someone with cluster-admin can still delete the binding by hand, use the `register-mgmt-cluster` token, or edit SveltosCluster labels.
- **C relies on a comment-only unit bundling cleanly.** If it does not, the harmless profile simply stays.
