## 5. Three ways out

**Fix the application.** Lower `spec.replicas` to at most 4 in `mer-kyverno-class-test/kyverno`, then re-run `cub sveltos impact` against the new revision. *Changes:* the candidate configuration only; the policies stay as they are. *Risks:* if the 6 replicas were asked for to handle real admission webhook load in test, this removes the reason for the change — the proposal comes back unresolved rather than settled. *A reviewer should check:* why 6 was proposed, and whether the test environment now takes enough admission traffic that its ceiling of 4 is the thing that is actually out of date.

**Correct the policy.** Raise `max` in the `replica-limits-test` ConfigMap inside `mer-policies/replica-limits`. *Changes:* the ceiling for every Deployment in every namespace labelled `impact.confighub.com/stage: test`, not just Kyverno's — the binding is namespace-scoped, not workload-scoped. It also creates a new revision of a unit whose `@2` is tagged `in-force`, so the policy in force for the fleet changes. *Risks:* a quiet, broad loosening of a stage's cost and blast-radius guard, made to unblock one Deployment. It would also invert the stage ordering — test's ceiling would sit above prod's 5. *A reviewer should check:* that the new number is deliberate for the whole stage, that the test/uat/prod ceilings still read as an intentional sequence afterwards, and that the change to the policy unit is reviewed as a policy change, not as part of the Kyverno rollout.

**Ask for an explicit exception.** Narrow `replica-limits-test` so this one Deployment is out of its scope — an object selector on the binding, or a match condition on the policy — leaving the ceiling of 4 in place for everything else in test. *Changes:* the policy unit again, but in a way that names what is exempt. *Risks:* an exemption is invisible at the point where someone later reads "test ceiling: 4" and believes it; exemptions accumulate. There is precedent in this slice for declining one — revision 3 of `disallow-latest-tag` proposed exempting Kyverno's own controllers and was not adopted, with the note *"Kyverno's controllers stay on pinned tags."* *A reviewer should check:* whether the same reasoning that rejected that exemption applies here, that the exception names the single Deployment rather than a class of workloads, and that it carries an owner and a reason someone can find later.
